# Ice study — reference revision

The three supplied ice photographs guide the irregular connected plate boundaries, milky frost grain, and white fracture lips. The gray-white color direction is retained. These are procedural shapes, not a photograph pasted over the interaction.

- Slide: fine scratches and loose frost particles.
- Hold: a growing set of unequal, jagged fracture branches starts at the contact.
- Continued pressure: nearby plate seams open progressively, and enclosed plates settle along their own irregular boundaries.
- Release: growth stops and damage remains. Snowflake resets the surface.
- Touch uses dwell time as a pressure proxy; pen pressure supplements it. This is an artistic approximation, not a physical ice simulation.

Geometry is shared by neighboring plates so their borders fit. Texture is cached, pixel ratio is capped, and released fracture clusters are bounded.

## Sound and contact revision

Sliding no longer draws a white scratch or throws ice particles. A broad, low-contrast damp impression fades within 1.4 seconds, with a subtle contact reflection. Pressure-driven fractures remain.

Audio uses real CC0 ice crushing and hand-on-windshield-frost recordings (see audio credits). Sliding speed controls quiet filtered rubbing excerpts. New fracture growth triggers short cracks with a global rate/voice cap; fully grown, stationary damage does not continue cracking indefinitely. First touch unlocks audio, and the music icon toggles mute. Phone pressure is still simulated from dwell time.

### Quieter audio revision
Rubbing now plays one continuous recording segment with movement-controlled gain, rather than overlapping 280ms grains. Its level is lower and its stereo position narrower. Each press has at most two load-threshold fracture events, alternating two CC0 ice takes with their recorded tails intact. Idle holds no longer replay a crack every 260–420ms. Perceived naturalness still needs listening feedback on the target phone.

### Faint writing through surface frost
A half-resolution accumulation mask now stores friction. Slow passes contribute more than fast passes; retracing builds visibility with a 25% opacity ceiling. Spatial variation produces uneven, feathered strokes. The revealed layer contains subdued moving underwater light, not a clear hole through the ice. Accumulated marks fade exponentially (about nine-second half-life). Only near-stationary contact builds fracture pressure; normal slow writing stays separate. Reset and resize clear the mask.
